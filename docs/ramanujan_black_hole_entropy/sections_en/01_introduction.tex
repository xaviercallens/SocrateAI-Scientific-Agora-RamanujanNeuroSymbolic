\section{Introduction}

The convergence of algebraic geometry and quantum information has opened new avenues for understanding macroscopic phenomena. Specifically, the relationship between Ramanujan's mock modular forms, the BPS state counts of K3 surface compactifications, and the emergence of macroscopic Fuzzy Dark Matter (FDM) forms the core of our Dual-Scale framework. In this paper, we consolidate the mathematical foundations of this relationship, verified computationally via Lean 4, and demonstrate its native mapping into holographic tensor networks. We emphasize from the outset a crucial phenomenological boundary condition: the microscopic states counted by this object apply strictly to supersymmetric extremal (BPS) configurations, rather than to non-extremal astrophysical targets.

By bypassing classical differential geometric approaches and utilizing exact rational arithmetic, we establish a rigid bridge from microscopic string states (UV) to observable macroscopic physics (IR).
